# Review batch 3: 100 prompts

Status: **approved by Stijn** (shown 15 November 2026). 100 prompts: 78 more from the Eurth forum's list, 22 new in the voice of batch 1.

They sit on 1 May to 8 August, the themes in turn, so no theme falls on two days running. Dates and numbers may still move before launch; the words are what to review.

78 of them come from the Eurth forum's own [Worldbuilding Prompts](https://eurth.org/t/worldbuilding-prompts/17685) list, which also holds the [daily prompts of December 2020](https://eurth.org/t/december-2020-event-daily-worldbuilding-prompts/3695). The forum's prompts often ask four questions at once; here each is cut to one or two sentences and 200 characters, in British spelling, with "your nation" throughout. Where a prompt leaned on the real world or on fantasy (Christmas and Halloween, a Kaiju attack, more than one moon), that part is left out. A few gained a second question in the voice of batch 1 ("and who wants them changed?").

## Politics

- **125**, 5 May: Describe the buildings your government sits in. Which of them was built to impress, and whom?
- **133**, 13 May: What powers does your police force have, and what holds it back?
- **141**, 21 May: Which international organisations is your nation a member of, and which one would it leave tomorrow if it could?
- **149**, 29 May: Who are your nation's allies and who are its enemies? Which of them used to be the other?
- **157**, 6 June: How does someone immigrate to your nation? Describe the paperwork, the wait and the part nobody warns them about.
- **165**, 14 June: How does your nation's main religion influence its laws and government?
- **173**, 22 June: How do your nation's founding myths turn up in the speeches of its politicians today?
- **181**, 30 June: Is there discrimination or segregation in your nation? If so, how do those who defend it justify it?
- **189**, 8 July: Is there a free press in your nation, or does the state decide what is news?
- **197**, 16 July: How are those who do not follow the main religion viewed and treated in your nation?
- **205**, 24 July: A law in your nation is broken by almost everyone, every day. Why is it still on the books?
- **213**, 1 August: Your capital and your largest city are run by rival parties. What do they fight over?

## History

- **126**, 6 May: How are your nation's historical traumas taught to each new generation?
- **134**, 14 May: What role have plagues played in your nation's history, and what did the last one change for good?
- **142**, 22 May: How do your nation's most important holidays reflect its historical triumphs and its traumas?
- **150**, 30 May: A treaty your nation signed generations ago still binds it. What did it give away, and who wants it torn up?
- **158**, 7 June: Your nation once had a different name. Why was it changed, and who still uses the old one?
- **166**, 15 June: A year is missing from your nation's official history. What happened in it?
- **174**, 23 June: Your nation's oldest law is still in force. What does it say, and when was it last used?
- **182**, 1 July: An invention your nation claims as its own is claimed by a neighbour too. What is it, and who is right?
- **190**, 9 July: Your nation's borders were drawn by someone who never saw them. Who, and what did they get wrong?
- **198**, 17 July: A revolution in your nation's past failed. What did the losers want, and which of their demands came true anyway?
- **206**, 25 July: A famine, a fire or a flood is remembered by a single name in your nation. What did the government do, or fail to do?
- **214**, 2 August: Your nation's last monarch, or its first president, left office badly. How?
