# Review batch 3: 100 prompts

Status: **approved by Stijn** (shown 15 November 2026). 100 prompts: 78 more from the Eurth forum's list, 22 new in the voice of batch 1.

Each prompt is listed with its number and its day. The days were shuffled on 15 November 2026, after the review, so a batch is spread over the year (this one from 21 January to 20 December); no theme falls on two days running.

78 of them come from the Eurth forum's own [Worldbuilding Prompts](https://eurth.org/t/worldbuilding-prompts/17685) list, which also holds the [daily prompts of December 2020](https://eurth.org/t/december-2020-event-daily-worldbuilding-prompts/3695). The forum's prompts often ask four questions at once; here each is cut to one or two sentences and 200 characters, in British spelling, with "your nation" throughout. Where a prompt leaned on the real world or on fantasy (Christmas and Halloween, a Kaiju attack, more than one moon), that part is left out. A few gained a second question in the voice of batch 1 ("and who wants them changed?").

## Politics

- **25**, 25 January: Describe the buildings your government sits in. Which of them was built to impress, and whom?
- **37**, 6 February: Is there discrimination or segregation in your nation? If so, how do those who defend it justify it?
- **61**, 2 March: Which international organisations is your nation a member of, and which one would it leave tomorrow if it could?
- **72**, 13 March: How are those who do not follow the main religion viewed and treated in your nation?
- **80**, 21 March: Who are your nation's allies and who are its enemies? Which of them used to be the other?
- **92**, 2 April: How do your nation's founding myths turn up in the speeches of its politicians today?
- **145**, 25 May: Is there a free press in your nation, or does the state decide what is news?
- **163**, 12 June: A law in your nation is broken by almost everyone, every day. Why is it still on the books?
- **248**, 5 September: Your capital and your largest city are run by rival parties. What do they fight over?
- **255**, 12 September: What powers does your police force have, and what holds it back?
- **290**, 17 October: How does your nation's main religion influence its laws and government?
- **315**, 11 November: How does someone immigrate to your nation? Describe the paperwork, the wait and the part nobody warns them about.

## History

- **21**, 21 January: A revolution in your nation's past failed. What did the losers want, and which of their demands came true anyway?
- **69**, 10 March: Your nation's last monarch, or its first president, left office badly. How?
- **100**, 10 April: A treaty your nation signed generations ago still binds it. What did it give away, and who wants it torn up?
- **167**, 16 June: An invention your nation claims as its own is claimed by a neighbour too. What is it, and who is right?
- **170**, 19 June: Your nation's borders were drawn by someone who never saw them. Who, and what did they get wrong?
- **176**, 25 June: Your nation's oldest law is still in force. What does it say, and when was it last used?
- **195**, 14 July: How do your nation's most important holidays reflect its historical triumphs and its traumas?
- **227**, 15 August: What role have plagues played in your nation's history, and what did the last one change for good?
- **239**, 27 August: A famine, a fire or a flood is remembered by a single name in your nation. What did the government do, or fail to do?
- **259**, 16 September: How are your nation's historical traumas taught to each new generation?
- **313**, 9 November: Your nation once had a different name. Why was it changed, and who still uses the old one?
- **325**, 21 November: A year is missing from your nation's official history. What happened in it?

- **108**, 18 April: Can you give a linguistic overview of your nation: which languages are spoken where, and which one do strangers use with each other?
- **147**, 27 May: What is the biggest, most beloved celebration of the year in your nation? I am invited: what should I wear?
- **165**, 14 June: What is the best food to eat in each region of your nation?
- **168**, 17 June: What are the different religions in your nation, and how do they treat each other?
- **180**, 29 June: What do your people believe happens after death, and who is promised the best of it?
- **252**, 9 September: Is a traditional art form in your nation losing out to a new entertainment? What does the quarrel say about the country?
- **264**, 21 September: How is the climate and farming of each region reflected in its traditional cooking?
- **267**, 24 September: What is the most distinctive symbol of your nation, the one most likely to be recognised abroad?
- **295**, 22 October: What are the greatest sins in your nation, and who decides?
- **334**, 30 November: What sort of architecture can a visitor expect in your nation? Describe a palace, a place of worship and a railway station.

- **121**, 1 May: Does your economic system reinforce the class structure of your nation, or challenge it?
- **131**, 11 May: Your nation's tax office is feared, mocked or ignored. Which, and what did it do to earn it?
- **138**, 18 May: What is the most valuable material in your nation, and how is it used?
- **162**, 11 June: Is there financial regulation in your nation, or is cronyism the standard practice?

- **199**, 18 July: A product is cheaper across the border, and everyone knows it. What is it, and how does it get home?
