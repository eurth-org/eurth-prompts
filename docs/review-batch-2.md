# Review batch 2: 100 prompts

Status: **approved by Stijn** (shown 15 November 2026). The Eurth forum's own prompts, cut to fit the card.

Each prompt is listed with its number and its day. The days were shuffled on 15 November 2026, after the review, so a batch is spread over the year (this one from 27 January to 18 December); no theme falls on two days running.

All of them come from the Eurth forum's own [Worldbuilding Prompts](https://eurth.org/t/worldbuilding-prompts/17685) list, which also holds the [daily prompts of December 2020](https://eurth.org/t/december-2020-event-daily-worldbuilding-prompts/3695). The forum's prompts often ask four questions at once; here each is cut to one or two sentences and 200 characters, in British spelling, with "your nation" throughout. Where a prompt leaned on the real world or on fantasy (Christmas and Halloween, a Kaiju attack, more than one moon), that part is left out. A few gained a second question in the voice of batch 1 ("and who wants them changed?").

## Politics

- **49**, 18 February: What could be found on your nation's black markets, and who looks the other way?
- **101**, 11 April: Which political parties exist in your nation, what divides them, and how do they work together?
- **110**, 20 April: Who is the leader of your nation, and how did they get into power?
- **127**, 7 May: Describe an election day in your nation. Who votes, where, and how long before anyone knows the result?
- **190**, 9 July: A thief steals a visitor's wallet in one of your cities. Who do they report it to, and what will be done about it?
- **202**, 21 July: What are the gun laws of your nation, and who wants them changed?
- **231**, 19 August: Can the courts strike down laws in your nation? Why, or why not?
- **271**, 28 September: What is your prison system for, rehabilitation or punishment? Describe an ordinary day inside.
- **281**, 8 October: In a leader, what do the people of your nation value most: strength, kindness or good sense? Who was the last leader to have it?
- **296**, 23 October: Is your civil service efficient and open to talent, or a bloated system of patronage? Say how someone really gets a job in it.
- **304**, 31 October: What is the single greatest threat your nation faces today: nature, politics or unrest? Who is paid to worry about it?
- **327**, 23 November: Who is your nation's closest ally, and why? What would it take to end the friendship?
- **343**, 9 December: What is the most significant foreign policy goal of your current government, and which neighbour stands in its way?

## History

- **33**, 2 February: How is history taught in your nation? Name one accepted truth that is actually a lie.
- **45**, 14 February: What is the great 'what if' of your nation's history? How would things be different had it gone the other way?
- **73**, 14 March: Describe a civil war in your nation's past. Who fought whom, and is it really over?
- **83**, 24 March: What skills or trades from your nation's history have died out? Is anyone trying to revive them?
- **129**, 9 May: What is the closest your nation has come to being wiped out, and what saved it?
- **136**, 16 May: From knights of virtue to pirates of the high seas: what kind of people has your nation romanticised, and what were they really like?
- **146**, 26 May: What is the most sought-after lost treasure of your nation's past, and who is still looking for it?
- **156**, 5 June: Are your people the original inhabitants of your nation's lands, or did they displace another group? Who remembers it differently?
- **204**, 23 July: Give the synopsis of your people's national epic. Which part does everyone know by heart?
- **215**, 3 August: How did your nation become independent, and from whom?
- **268**, 25 September: What are the most famous ruins in your nation? What were they in their glory days, and why are they in ruin?
- **280**, 7 October: Which landmark in your nation was built by an ancient culture for reasons nobody now knows? What is the favourite theory?
- **318**, 14 November: What is considered the darkest day in your nation's history, and how is it remembered?

## Culture

- **47**, 16 February: When do your people celebrate the new year, and what does it involve?
- **70**, 11 March: How do your people think the world began, and how do they think it will end?
- **87**, 28 March: Give a proverb or two from your nation. What do they say about the people who use them?
- **97**, 7 April: What are the best-known folk stories in your nation? Who brings good fortune, and who is the bogeyman parents scare their children with?
- **122**, 2 May: What sorts of headgear do people wear in your nation, and why: use, ceremony or prestige?
- **134**, 14 May: What makes people laugh in your nation: dry wit, sarcasm, slapstick? Which subjects are never joked about?
- **201**, 20 July: Does your government fund the arts? If so, what strings come attached?
- **209**, 28 July: Which film was the latest box office success in your nation? What was it about, and why was it so popular?
- **234**, 22 August: What is the traditional formal wear of your nation, and what do people wear to relax? Which outfit might be called the national dress?
- **287**, 14 October: What symbolism do colours have in your nation's cultures? Which colour would nobody wear to a wedding?
- **307**, 3 November: What do your people consider to be art, and what do they refuse to call art?
- **326**, 22 November: What sort of music is popular in your nation? Is there a genre your nation created or made its own?
- **352**, 18 December: How do the people of your nation spend the days when they are not working? How long is the weekend, and who gets it off?

## Economy

- **30**, 30 January: What are the most common recreational drugs in your nation, and which of them are legal?
- **42**, 11 February: What happens to rubbish and sewage in your nation? Where does it all end up?
- **68**, 9 March: I am searching for work in your nation. Where could I apply, where would I apply, and where should I?
- **79**, 20 March: How easy is it to start a business in your nation? Name what stands in the way.
- **91**, 1 April: What is your nation's largest export, and who would be ruined if the world stopped buying it?
- **117**, 27 April: What do your nation's farmers grow, and which crop pays the bills?
- **143**, 23 May: How do your people prefer to get around? How does it differ between the city and the countryside?
- **158**, 7 June: How wide is the gap between rich and poor in your nation? What catches those who fall on hard times?
- **245**, 2 September: What are the railways like in your nation? Are they part of a wider network, or cut off by gauge, geography or politics?
- **251**, 8 September: What is the currency of your nation? How did it come to be used, and what came before it?
- **258**, 15 September: What forms of gambling are legal in your nation? Who controls the gambling scene, and what does the public think of it?
- **308**, 4 November: Beyond the black market, what does the grey economy look like in your nation: street vendors, cash in hand, favours? How large is it?
- **336**, 2 December: Where does the drinking water come from in your nation, and how does it reach the tap?

- **34**, 3 February: How many branches do your armed forces have, and which formation in them is the most famous?
- **52**, 21 February: How are veterans treated after their service in your nation: honoured, or forgotten?
- **60**, 1 March: Which fort or castle in your nation matters most? Who built it, and against whom?
- **75**, 16 March: Which battle does your nation remember above all others? Who fought it, why, and how was it won?
- **142**, 22 May: Does your nation have an intelligence agency? Name its most famous success, and the failure it never mentions.
- **208**, 27 July: Describe the uniforms of your armed forces. What does a soldier wear on parade that would be useless in the field?
- **220**, 8 August: Who are the most feared enemies in your nation's history, and are they still feared?
- **230**, 18 August: Who guards your head of state? Describe the guard, its uniform and its oldest privilege.
- **254**, 11 September: Describe a day in the life of an ordinary soldier in training in your nation.
- **257**, 14 September: How long have your people known peace? How has war, or the lack of it, shaped your modern history?
- **269**, 26 September: Where does your nation stand on nuclear weapons? Who decided, and when?

## Geography

- **66**, 7 March: What does an ordinary public park look like in your nation? Is there wilderness left, and who protects it?
- **128**, 8 May: How is the ownership of land divided in your nation? Are there lands beyond your borders that your people still call theirs?
- **139**, 19 May: I have spent some time in your capital and decide to travel to the countryside. How different is life there?
- **151**, 31 May: What does the night sky look like from your nation, and what do your people call the shapes they see in it?
- **191**, 10 July: What are your nation's famous cities, apart from the capital, and what makes each unlike the others?
- **203**, 22 July: What lies beneath your nation: caves, underground rivers, old mines? Does anyone live or work down there?
- **253**, 10 September: What are the most important regions of your nation? What makes their people stand out from the rest?

- **278**, 5 October: How did your nation decide which city was to be its capital?
- **291**, 18 October: Tourists travel from one end of your nation to the other. Which five natural wonders do they stop for?

- **309**, 5 November: Which body of water is the most indispensable in your nation? How has access to water, or the lack of it, featured in your history?
