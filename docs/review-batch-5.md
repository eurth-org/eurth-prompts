# Review batch 5: 31 prompts

Status: **approved by Stijn** (shown 15 November 2026). The last 31, with the leap day's prompt: 1 from the Eurth forum's list, 30 new.

They sit on 29 February to 21 December, the themes in turn, so no theme falls on two days running. Dates and numbers may still move before launch; the words are what to review.

1 of them come from the Eurth forum's own [Worldbuilding Prompts](https://eurth.org/t/worldbuilding-prompts/17685) list, which also holds the [daily prompts of December 2020](https://eurth.org/t/december-2020-event-daily-worldbuilding-prompts/3695). The forum's prompts often ask four questions at once; here each is cut to one or two sentences and 200 characters, in British spelling, with "your nation" throughout. Where a prompt leaned on the real world or on fantasy (Christmas and Halloween, a Kaiju attack, more than one moon), that part is left out. A few gained a second question in the voice of batch 1 ("and who wants them changed?").

## Politics

- **366**, 29 February: Something in your nation happens only once every four years: an election, a census, a games. What is it, and what do people do in the years between?
- **325**, 21 November: The national anthem has a verse that is no longer sung. What does it say?
- **333**, 29 November: An ambassador has just been sent home from your capital. Whose, and what was the official reason?
- **341**, 7 December: Your nation gives the vote at an age its neighbours think odd. What age, and who argued for it?

## History

- **326**, 22 November: A great building in your nation was never finished. Who ran out of money, patience or time?
- **334**, 30 November: A day in your calendar marks a defeat, not a victory. Why is it kept?

## Culture

- **327**, 23 November: One regional accent in your nation is used on stage to mean 'fool'. Whose, and how do they take it?
- **335**, 1 December: How do the values taught in your nation's schools show up in its art and culture?
- **343**, 9 December: A dance in your nation was once forbidden. By whom, and where is it danced now?

## Economy

- **328**, 24 November: Your nation's pension age has just changed. Which way, and who was in the streets about it?
- **336**, 2 December: A harvest failed in your nation last year. Of what, and what did people go without?
- **352**, 18 December: Electricity in your nation comes mostly from one source. Which, and what happens when it fails?

## Military

- **321**, 17 November: Your nation's defence ministry occupies a building it has outgrown, or one far too grand. Which, and what does that say?
- **329**, 25 November: A song the soldiers sing is not in any official songbook. What is it about?
- **337**, 3 December: Your nation's war graves lie partly in someone else's country. Who tends them?
- **353**, 19 December: The armed forces run something that has nothing to do with war: a bakery, an airline, a football club. What, and why?

## Geography

- **322**, 18 November: Your nation's highest point is not its most famous mountain. Which is, and why?
- **330**, 26 November: One crop, tree or animal is found on your coat of arms and hardly anywhere else. Where did it go?
- **338**, 4 December: A new town in your nation was built from nothing in ten years. Who moved there, and who refused?
- **349**, 15 December: Your nation's longest tunnel, bridge or road joins two places that had managed without it. What changed when it opened?
- **354**, 20 December: The place where your nation's three largest regions meet is marked by something. What, and who visits?

## Daily life

- **323**, 19 November: There is one day a year when nobody in your nation works. What do they do instead?
- **331**, 27 November: A neighbour in your nation is too loud, too nosy or too quiet. How is that dealt with?
- **339**, 5 December: What does it cost to get married, to be buried and to be born in your nation? Which is dearest?
- **350**, 16 December: How do people in your nation greet a friend, a stranger and a superior? What goes wrong when they mix them up?
- **355**, 21 December: What is sold at a street corner in your nation that is sold nowhere else?

## Characters

- **324**, 20 November: A shopkeeper in your capital has sold to every head of government for decades. What do they know about each?
- **332**, 28 November: The most hated referee, critic or tax inspector in your nation has a name everyone knows. What did they do?
- **340**, 6 December: A poet in your nation is quoted by every side in every argument. Which line, and what did it mean?
- **342**, 8 December: Someone in your nation holds an ancient office with a fine title and no duties. What is it, and what do they do all day?
- **351**, 17 December: A scientist in your nation refused a prize. Which prize, and why?
