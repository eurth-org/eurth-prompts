# Review batch 5: 31 prompts

Status: **approved by Stijn** (shown 15 November 2026). The last 31, with the leap day's prompt: 1 from the Eurth forum's list, 30 new.

Each prompt is listed with its number and its day. The days were shuffled on 15 November 2026, after the review, so a batch is spread over the year (this one from 23 January to 29 November); no theme falls on two days running.

1 of them come from the Eurth forum's own [Worldbuilding Prompts](https://eurth.org/t/worldbuilding-prompts/17685) list, which also holds the [daily prompts of December 2020](https://eurth.org/t/december-2020-event-daily-worldbuilding-prompts/3695). The forum's prompts often ask four questions at once; here each is cut to one or two sentences and 200 characters, in British spelling, with "your nation" throughout. Where a prompt leaned on the real world or on fantasy (Christmas and Halloween, a Kaiju attack, more than one moon), that part is left out. A few gained a second question in the voice of batch 1 ("and who wants them changed?").

## Politics

- **366**, 29 February: Something in your nation happens only once every four years: an election, a census, a games. What is it, and what do people do in the years between?
- **95**, 5 April: An ambassador has just been sent home from your capital. Whose, and what was the official reason?
- **178**, 27 June: Your nation gives the vote at an age its neighbours think odd. What age, and who argued for it?
- **333**, 29 November: The national anthem has a verse that is no longer sung. What does it say?

## History

- **112**, 22 April: A great building in your nation was never finished. Who ran out of money, patience or time?
- **123**, 3 May: A day in your calendar marks a defeat, not a victory. Why is it kept?

## Culture

- **154**, 3 June: How do the values taught in your nation's schools show up in its art and culture?
- **198**, 17 July: A dance in your nation was once forbidden. By whom, and where is it danced now?
- **243**, 31 August: One regional accent in your nation is used on stage to mean 'fool'. Whose, and how do they take it?

## Economy

- **186**, 5 July: Electricity in your nation comes mostly from one source. Which, and what happens when it fails?
- **305**, 1 November: A harvest failed in your nation last year. Of what, and what did people go without?
- **324**, 20 November: Your nation's pension age has just changed. Which way, and who was in the streets about it?

## Military

- **130**, 10 May: A song the soldiers sing is not in any official songbook. What is it about?
- **188**, 7 July: Your nation's war graves lie partly in someone else's country. Who tends them?
- **276**, 3 October: Your nation's defence ministry occupies a building it has outgrown, or one far too grand. Which, and what does that say?
- **328**, 24 November: The armed forces run something that has nothing to do with war: a bakery, an airline, a football club. What, and why?

## Geography

- **26**, 26 January: The place where your nation's three largest regions meet is marked by something. What, and who visits?
- **48**, 17 February: Your nation's longest tunnel, bridge or road joins two places that had managed without it. What changed when it opened?
- **111**, 21 April: A new town in your nation was built from nothing in ten years. Who moved there, and who refused?
- **241**, 29 August: Your nation's highest point is not its most famous mountain. Which is, and why?
- **270**, 27 September: One crop, tree or animal is found on your coat of arms and hardly anywhere else. Where did it go?

## Daily life

- **152**, 1 June: A neighbour in your nation is too loud, too nosy or too quiet. How is that dealt with?
- **224**, 12 August: There is one day a year when nobody in your nation works. What do they do instead?
- **232**, 20 August: How do people in your nation greet a friend, a stranger and a superior? What goes wrong when they mix them up?
- **261**, 18 September: What is sold at a street corner in your nation that is sold nowhere else?
- **314**, 10 November: What does it cost to get married, to be buried and to be born in your nation? Which is dearest?

## Characters

- **23**, 23 January: A shopkeeper in your capital has sold to every head of government for decades. What do they know about each?
- **225**, 13 August: Someone in your nation holds an ancient office with a fine title and no duties. What is it, and what do they do all day?
- **256**, 13 September: A poet in your nation is quoted by every side in every argument. Which line, and what did it mean?
- **263**, 20 September: The most hated referee, critic or tax inspector in your nation has a name everyone knows. What did they do?
- **302**, 29 October: A scientist in your nation refused a prize. Which prize, and why?
