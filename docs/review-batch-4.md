# Review batch 4: 100 prompts

Status: **waiting for Stijn's review** (shown 15 November 2026). 100 prompts: 13 from the Eurth forum's list, 87 new in the voice of batch 1.

They sit on 9 August to 16 November, the themes in turn, so no theme falls on two days running. Dates and numbers may still move before launch; the words are what to review.

13 of them come from the Eurth forum's own [Worldbuilding Prompts](https://eurth.org/t/worldbuilding-prompts/17685) list, which also holds the [daily prompts of December 2020](https://eurth.org/t/december-2020-event-daily-worldbuilding-prompts/3695). The forum's prompts often ask four questions at once; here each is cut to one or two sentences and 200 characters, in British spelling, with "your nation" throughout. Where a prompt leaned on the real world or on fantasy (Christmas and Halloween, a Kaiju attack, more than one moon), that part is left out. A few gained a second question in the voice of batch 1 ("and who wants them changed?").

## Politics

- **221**, 9 August: Your nation's constitution has one article nobody dares to amend. What does it say, and what happened the last time someone tried?
- **229**, 17 August: A minister in your government has resigned over something small. What was it, and what was it really about?
- **237**, 25 August: One town in your nation has had the same mayor for thirty years. How, and who has stopped trying to beat them?
- **245**, 2 September: Your parliament once sat through the night to pass a single bill. What was so urgent?
- **253**, 10 September: An election in your nation was decided by a handful of votes. Which one, and does the losing side still say it was stolen?
- **261**, 18 September: A protest in your capital has gone on for so long that it has become a landmark. What do the protesters want?
- **269**, 26 September: Your nation's smallest party holds the balance of power. What does it ask for, every time?
- **277**, 4 October: Your government keeps a list of things that may not be exported. What is the strangest item on it?
- **285**, 12 October: A referendum in your nation gave the wrong answer. What was the question, and what did the government do next?
- **293**, 20 October: Your nation's upper house, council or court is full of people nobody elected. Who put them there, and why is that tolerated?
- **301**, 28 October: A census in your nation asks one question that people lie about. Which, and why?
- **309**, 5 November: A scandal ended three careers in your government last year. Who talked?
- **317**, 13 November: Your nation's local councils have one power the capital envies. What is it?

## History

- **222**, 10 August: Your nation's founding document is kept behind glass. What is missing from it, and who noticed?
- **230**, 18 August: A city in your nation was once the capital of somewhere else. What is left of that time?
- **238**, 26 August: Your nation's oldest university was founded for a purpose it no longer serves. What was it?
- **246**, 3 September: A language once spoken in your nation survives only in place names. Who spoke it, and where did they go?
- **254**, 11 September: A ship, a train or a plane was lost in your nation's past and never found. What was aboard?
- **262**, 19 September: Your nation changed its flag once. What was wrong with the old one, and who still flies it?
- **270**, 27 September: A king, a president or a general in your past is remembered by a nickname. What is it, and did they earn it?
- **278**, 5 October: Your nation's first railway, road or canal went somewhere odd. Where, and who insisted?
- **286**, 13 October: A trial in your nation's past is still argued over. Who was in the dock, and was the verdict just?
- **294**, 21 October: Your grandparents' generation lived through something they called simply 'the hard years'. What happened?
- **302**, 29 October: A border your nation once had runs through the middle of a modern town. How can a visitor still tell?
- **310**, 6 November: A book was banned in your nation for a century. What was in it, and who read it anyway?
- **318**, 14 November: Your nation once had colonies, or was one. What is the one word from that time that everybody still uses?

## Economy

- **304**, 31 October: Shops in your nation close on a particular day or hour that annoys visitors. When, and who defends it?
- **312**, 8 November: Your nation's poorest region sits on its richest resource. How did that happen?
