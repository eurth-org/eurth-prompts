# Review batch 4: 100 prompts

Status: **approved by Stijn** (shown 15 November 2026). 100 prompts: 13 from the Eurth forum's list, 87 new in the voice of batch 1.

Each prompt is listed with its number and its day. The days were shuffled on 15 November 2026, after the review, so a batch is spread over the year (this one from 24 January to 21 December); no theme falls on two days running.

13 of them come from the Eurth forum's own [Worldbuilding Prompts](https://eurth.org/t/worldbuilding-prompts/17685) list, which also holds the [daily prompts of December 2020](https://eurth.org/t/december-2020-event-daily-worldbuilding-prompts/3695). The forum's prompts often ask four questions at once; here each is cut to one or two sentences and 200 characters, in British spelling, with "your nation" throughout. Where a prompt leaned on the real world or on fantasy (Christmas and Halloween, a Kaiju attack, more than one moon), that part is left out. A few gained a second question in the voice of batch 1 ("and who wants them changed?").

## Politics

- **41**, 10 February: Your parliament once sat through the night to pass a single bill. What was so urgent?
- **65**, 6 March: Your nation's upper house, council or court is full of people nobody elected. Who put them there, and why is that tolerated?
- **120**, 30 April: One town in your nation has had the same mayor for thirty years. How, and who has stopped trying to beat them?
- **133**, 13 May: A minister in your government has resigned over something small. What was it, and what was it really about?
- **148**, 28 May: A protest in your capital has gone on for so long that it has become a landmark. What do the protesters want?
- **155**, 4 June: A scandal ended three careers in your government last year. Who talked?
- **175**, 24 June: A census in your nation asks one question that people lie about. Which, and why?
- **207**, 26 July: Your government keeps a list of things that may not be exported. What is the strangest item on it?
- **219**, 7 August: Your nation's constitution has one article nobody dares to amend. What does it say, and what happened the last time someone tried?
- **235**, 23 August: An election in your nation was decided by a handful of votes. Which one, and does the losing side still say it was stolen?
- **242**, 30 August: Your nation's local councils have one power the capital envies. What is it?
- **265**, 22 September: Your nation's smallest party holds the balance of power. What does it ask for, every time?
- **338**, 4 December: A referendum in your nation gave the wrong answer. What was the question, and what did the government do next?

## History

- **29**, 29 January: Your grandparents' generation lived through something they called simply 'the hard years'. What happened?
- **36**, 5 February: A city in your nation was once the capital of somewhere else. What is left of that time?
- **57**, 26 February: Your nation's oldest university was founded for a purpose it no longer serves. What was it?
- **90**, 31 March: Your nation changed its flag once. What was wrong with the old one, and who still flies it?
- **109**, 19 April: A language once spoken in your nation survives only in place names. Who spoke it, and where did they go?
- **119**, 29 April: Your nation's first railway, road or canal went somewhere odd. Where, and who insisted?
- **183**, 2 July: A trial in your nation's past is still argued over. Who was in the dock, and was the verdict just?
- **200**, 19 July: A king, a president or a general in your past is remembered by a nickname. What is it, and did they earn it?
- **223**, 11 August: A book was banned in your nation for a century. What was in it, and who read it anyway?
- **247**, 4 September: Your nation once had colonies, or was one. What is the one word from that time that everybody still uses?
- **292**, 19 October: A border your nation once had runs through the middle of a modern town. How can a visitor still tell?
- **301**, 28 October: A ship, a train or a plane was lost in your nation's past and never found. What was aboard?
- **331**, 27 November: Your nation's founding document is kept behind glass. What is missing from it, and who noticed?

## Culture

- **24**, 24 January: Your national theatre, opera or museum is fighting over one thing in its collection. What is it, and who wants it back?
- **32**, 1 February: There is a number, a colour or a day your people consider unlucky. Which, and how far do they go to avoid it?
- **55**, 24 February: What are the most popular forms of entertainment in your nation: which shows, books or games does everyone know?
- **77**, 18 March: People in your nation give a particular gift when someone moves house. What is it, and what does it mean to arrive without one?
- **115**, 25 April: A children's rhyme in your nation is about something terrible. What?
- **137**, 17 May: A word in your language cannot be translated. What does it mean, and when is it used?
- **192**, 11 July: What are the holy texts of your nation's religions, and who is allowed to interpret them?
- **218**, 6 August: Which holidays does your nation observe, and what is eaten or drunk only on days of celebration?
- **226**, 14 August: Which sports are popular in your nation, and is any of them dangerous enough to worry the authorities?
- **275**, 2 October: Which holy days does your nation keep, and what happens on the most solemn of them?
- **316**, 12 November: What do your people find fashionable? What is the latest trend, and which generation hates it?
- **340**, 6 December: Broken down to its essence, what does your nation's biggest religion believe?
- **355**, 21 December: Your nation has a gesture that means the opposite of what foreigners assume. What is it, and who found out the hard way?

## Economy

- **51**, 20 February: One import is taxed so heavily that smuggling it is a trade of its own. What is it?
- **56**, 25 February: Your nation's best-known brand is known abroad for the wrong reason. What is it, and what is the reason?
- **74**, 15 March: People in your nation save for one thing above all others. What is it, and at what age do they give up?
- **103**, 13 April: A motorway, a dam or an airport in your nation was built for traffic that never came. Who signed it off?
- **106**, 16 April: One profession in your nation is paid far more than it deserves, and one far less. Which are they?
- **149**, 29 May: A strike in your nation shut something down for a month. What, and who gave in first?
- **174**, 23 June: Your nation prints, mints or codes its own money. Who decides how much, and who complains?
- **210**, 29 July: Your nation subsidises something it cannot afford to. What, and who would riot if it stopped?
- **213**, 1 August: A family firm in your nation has outlived three governments and two currencies. What does it make?
- **222**, 10 August: Shops in your nation close on a particular day or hour that annoys visitors. When, and who defends it?
- **237**, 25 August: A whole region of your nation lives off visitors. What do the locals do in the off season?
- **262**, 19 September: Your nation's largest market sells one thing that is not strictly legal. What, and why do the police shop there too?
- **282**, 9 October: Your nation's poorest region sits on its richest resource. How did that happen?

## Military

- **63**, 4 March: Your nation keeps a reserve of ordinary citizens who could be called up. How many would actually come?
- **86**, 27 March: Your armed forces train every year for a war they hope never to fight. Against whom?
- **89**, 30 March: Your nation buys its fighter aircraft, rifles or ships from a single supplier. What does the supplier get in return?
- **99**, 9 April: A warship, a tank or an aircraft stands on a plinth in one of your towns. What did it do to earn it?
- **124**, 4 May: Your nation's border guards know the smugglers by name. How does that arrangement work?
- **150**, 30 May: Your nation's soldiers serve abroad under someone else's flag. Where, and what do they write home about?
- **182**, 1 July: One piece of your nation's coast, border or airspace is watched more closely than the rest. Why there?
- **196**, 15 July: A military parade is held in your capital once a year. What is shown, and what is kept out of sight?
- **293**, 20 October: A mutiny in your nation's history is taught as a heroic act, or not taught at all. Which, and why?
- **310**, 6 November: A general in your nation's past disobeyed an order and was proved right. What was the order?
- **335**, 1 December: Your nation's oldest regiment has a mascot, a motto or a custom it will not give up. What is it?

## Geography

- **88**, 29 March: Two towns in your nation face each other across a river and cannot stand each other. What started it?
- **96**, 6 April: The wind in your nation has a name. When does it blow, and what is it blamed for?
- **102**, 12 April: An island, an enclave or a valley in your nation runs on its own rules. Which rules, and who lets it?
- **144**, 24 May: A stretch of your nation's coast, steppe or desert is empty on purpose. Who emptied it, and why?
- **169**, 18 June: A village in your nation can only be reached by boat, by rail or on foot. Why has nobody built a road?
- **233**, 21 August: Your capital stands where it does for a reason that no longer applies. What was the reason?
- **283**, 10 October: A lake, a forest or a mountain in your nation is said to be cursed, haunted or holy. What do people actually avoid doing there?
- **299**, 26 October: One of your nation's borders is a line on a map and nothing on the ground. Who crosses it without noticing?
- **323**, 19 November: Your nation has a place everybody claims to have visited and few have. Where is it, and why do they lie?
- **332**, 28 November: Which is the most impressive building in your nation, and what was it built to prove?
- **342**, 8 December: A road in your nation is closed for half the year. What lies at the end of it, and who lives there through the winter?
